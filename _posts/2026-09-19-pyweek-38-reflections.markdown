pyweek38, a Python based gamejam held bi-annually, was the first game jam I competed in, held last year in September 2025. I entered as a solo team for practice purposes, devoting time outside of graduate studies to the experience. My goal for the exercise was to play with a variety of tech while still producing a playable game.

I used diffusion models and some approaches I picked up from O'Reilly's *Hands-On Generative AI with Transformers and Diffusion Models*, which I had been reading at the time, to produce character pixel art according to hand drawn sketches and styling. One of the best parts of graduate school was the free O'Reilly learning platform subscription. This pixel art became the basis of the images I used for cut scenes in the game. I also utilized the publicly available Grimes voice audio to create music for the game - in the end, she sang a soft, traditional song about the river, a central spect to my game and pyweek38 theme. 

The theme of pyweek38 was 'down stream,' and I - and most other participants - settled on a simple navigating obstactles type game with a literal river as the main graphic. Logging was a strong second theme present in several games, a particular stand out I enjoyed was a money management system based around logs flowing down the river model from the "logging site" to the "mill." After completing the main game with three levels and animating the background water, I packaged the final executable for Linux, Mac and Windows and ran tests on all platforms. My game scored highest in production, however it lacked in fun since it ended up being extremely similar to a number of other games in the competition, and lacked the polish and attention to graphics of others.

The majority of the time I spent programming different demos and testing various libraries, rather than on polishing the game appearance or concept. This achieved the goal I had set out, and I was happy to share something playable with the free time I had outside of my studies. 

I was left wanting to participate again, with the aim of focusing on the art of the game itself rather than as a personal learning endeavor. Looking forward to future gamejams and pyweek in particular since Python is such a fun language to use.
